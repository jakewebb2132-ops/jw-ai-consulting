# Strategic Portfolio Management (SPM)
**Source**: [[2026-10-25-planview-spm-pom-research]]
**Last updated**: 2026-10-26

## What it is
The enterprise decision system that **chooses and governs the bets**: strategy, objectives,
portfolio trade-offs, funding, capacity, risk, dependencies, and rebalancing.

SPM answers one question: *are we funding the right things, and what must move when that
changes?* Its unit of work is investments, portfolios, capital and capacity. Horizon is
quarters to years, reviewed monthly. Its decision rights are start, stop, defer, fund,
refund, reallocate.

## What it is not
**Not status reporting.** A portfolio body that only reports after decisions are locked is
governance theatre, not a decision system. The diagnostic question that exposes this: *when
did a portfolio review last stop something?*

It is also not replaced by [[product-operating-model]]. POM is how durable teams organize to
realize the bets SPM selects and funds.

## The three SPM stages in the strategy-to-value loop
1. **Strategic intent** — CEO/CFO/CSO. Annual direction, quarterly refresh. Turns ambition
   into a small set of measurable outcomes with owners, baselines, targets and horizons.
2. **Portfolio choices** — EPMO/Transformation Office. Monthly review, quarterly commitment.
   Compares live scenarios against shared criteria and real capacity. Every yes names the
   work it displaces.
3. **Investment and capacity** — CFO/Finance/Portfolio. Quarterly rolling forecast.
   Connects money *and people* to chosen bets while preserving the ability to move both.

The loop does not close until delivery evidence reaches stages 2 and 3 and changes them.
See [[spm-pom-strategy-to-value]] for the full ten-stage model.

## Funding models
Funding can follow projects, products, teams, value streams or outcomes. The direction of
travel is toward funding that can be re-pointed without a reorganization. Do not pretend
every enterprise uses one model: most run a hybrid, and regulated or capital-intensive
programs legitimately stay time-bound.

The failure mode is annual project budgets that lock scope, dates and team shape before any
evidence exists, after which sunk cost protects the weakest bets.

## Evidence

**Economist Impact, "Bridging the Gap: Turning Strategy into Reality"** (600 executives, 7
countries, 6 industries; commissioned by [[planview]]):
- 85% of executives say their organization's ability to adapt to change falls short.
- 86% say they need to improve accountability in strategy implementation.
- 84% recognize the need to improve data-driven decision-making.
- 20% are confident in the resources allocated to strategy implementation. *The funding
  model, not the strategy, is usually the constraint.*

**Strategy execution leadership research** (from the [[planview]] SPM solution page):
- Strategy Execution Leaders are 9.5× more likely to adapt quickly to market change.
- 91% of Strategy Execution Leaders grew revenue vs 61% of laggards.
- Reviewing strategy monthly rather than annually correlates with being 45% more likely to
  respond to change.
- Portfolio tool users reported 79% good-to-excellent visibility vs 66% of non-users.
- Organizations using these tools exceeded revenue targets 1.6× more often.
- Strategy Execution Leaders were 8× more likely to use AI across the organization.

## The 2026 category bar
Analyst criteria have moved from visibility to decision support. Forrester's Q2 2026 Wave
weighted strategic planning, scenario comparison and portfolio analysis, business
architecture and capability-based planning, and goal measurement and alignment. The vendor
argument is that a platform must convert data into a decision before the strategic window
closes: *"by the time a portfolio review surfaces a misalignment, the window to
course-correct has often already closed."*
