# Product Operating Model (POM)
**Source**: [[2026-10-25-planview-spm-pom-research]]
**Last updated**: 2026-10-25

## What it is
How an enterprise **organizes durable ownership** so that teams can realize the bets
[[strategic-portfolio-management]] selects and funds: value streams, products, empowered
cross-functional teams, outcome roadmaps, and decision rights.

POM answers: *who durably owns this value, and with what autonomy?* Its unit of work is
value streams, products, teams and decision rights. Horizon is persistent, with boundaries
reviewed semiannually.

## What it is not
**POM does not replace SPM.** This is the most common misread. SPM chooses and funds; POM
owns and realizes. An organization that adopts POM vocabulary without changing how capital
is allocated has renamed its project teams.

It also does not eliminate program management. Real enterprises run a hybrid: long-lived
products alongside time-bound programs, especially under regulatory or capital constraints.

## The structural change
The hinge is **value streams**. Organizing around persistent customer or business value
rather than temporary projects is the structural change POM makes, and the one most
organizations skip. The tell: funding lands on a project instead of a value stream, and
everything downstream becomes theatre.

Three POM stages in the strategy-to-value loop:
1. **Value streams** — business and product leadership. Persistent. Named customers, clear
   boundaries, own measures, own funding, accountable leader.
2. **Product strategy** — product leader/manager. Continuous, reviewed quarterly. Outcome
   roadmap of problems and hypotheses, not a promised feature list.
3. **Durable teams** — product, engineering, design as one team. Persistent. Bounded
   mission, measurable outcomes, full discovery-to-run lifecycle. *Teams stay; work changes.*

## Diagnostic heuristics
- **Team stability:** how many initiatives is the average engineer named on? Above ~1.5, the
  organization is funding projects and calling it product.
- **Decision rights:** who can say no to a senior stakeholder request? If nobody below the
  executive sponsor, product management is intake coordination.
- **Roadmap shape:** organized by features and dates, or by problems and outcomes?

## Evidence
From the Planview 2024 Project to Product State of the Industry (8,000 value streams;
"elite" = meets quarterly business objectives >90% of the time):

- Elite orgs are **2× more likely** to leverage a POM (50% vs 25%).
- Elite orgs are **3× more likely** to operate the entire enterprise as a Value Stream Network.
- Elite orgs are **4× more likely** to make deliverables and dependencies visible to teams.
- Low performers have **3× more wasted development work**.
- Only **12%** have fully shifted to a POM; **97%** report at least one roadblock.
- **65%** cannot see their organization as a Value Stream Network.
- Only **10%** have structured dependency management across teams.
- Only **15%** can incorporate customer feedback within weeks.
- **50%** expect 80% of their work to be product-based within five years.
- Burnout: only 13% reported no symptoms; 65% of those came from higher performers.

## Kersten's framing
> "A project-oriented approach focuses on funding milestones, defined project timelines,
> cost center metrics, and delivery of requirements. In contrast, a product-oriented
> approach focuses on funding value streams, product lifecycles, profit center metrics,
> and delivery of business value."

## Measurement
Planview frames POM success around five metric categories rather than structural pillars:
Strategic Portfolio, Value Stream, Agile Development, Product Success, and Financial &
DevOps metrics. The value stream layer is [[flow-framework]].

Caution for customer conversations: **output telemetry is not outcome evidence.** Deploy
frequency tells you the factory works. Flow distribution tells you what the factory chose
to build. Only adoption, business value, cost and risk tell you whether it mattered.
